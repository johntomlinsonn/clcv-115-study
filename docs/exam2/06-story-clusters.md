## e2c1 — Demeter, Persephone & Eleusis (Powell 10 · Sept 29)
- **Demeter** = fertility goddess of crops (grain), mother of **Persephone** (also called **Korê**, "daughter/girl"; Roman Ceres / Proserpina). Name: *-mētēr* "mother", *De-* unclear. Images: grain, sceptre, sometimes torch, basket. Shrines: **Eleusis** (near Athens), Sicily. Few stories: Arion (fast horse, with Poseidon), Erysichthon (offends her, punished), Persephone.
- **Kinds of fertility** (CRQ): Artemis — childbirth / wild animals & young; Aphrodite — sexual desire; Hera — marriage; Demeter — crops. *(Powell — confirm wording)*
- **Olympian goddesses & marriage:** 50/50 split — unmarried **Hestia, Athena, Artemis** (virginity = independence/power) vs. married **Hera, Demeter, Aphrodite**. Integrating older goddesses into a patriarchy = replace (Themis at Delphi → Apollo), demote (local goddesses "marry" Zeus), or combine (Ephesian + Olympian Artemis).
- **Hestia (Vesta):** name = hearth; cooked food; gets part of every sacrifice; unmarried; veiled; barely anthropomorphized; opposite of Hermes (first/last born, interior/exterior). Rome: Vesta's six Vestal priestesses.
- **Homeric Hymn to Demeter — the wrath story:**
  - *Offense:* Hades abducts Persephone (picking a **narcissus**) with Zeus's OK; Demeter excluded; no gifts (≠ normal Greek marriage: father arranges, procession, dowry). **Helios (Sun) sees all**; Hecate heard.
  - *Anger:* Demeter's rage → **famine** (her power; cf. flood ← Zeus, disease ← Apollo/Artemis, earthquakes ← Poseidon, madness ← Dionysus, infatuation ← Aphrodite). Crisis in the divine order: she ignores Zeus.
  - *Appeasement:* Zeus gives in, sends **Hermes** to bring her back; but Hades tricks her into eating a **pomegranate seed** = **food of the dead** (cf. lotus eaters; *xenia* bond) → Persephone spends part of the year below. Demeter restores crops and founds the **Eleusinian Mysteries**.
  - *Allegory* (characters & events = symbols): Persephone = annual crops / "dying god" (vegetation) / a girl's (parthenos) sexual maturation & marriage — "the other side of Demeter". Oath "by the two goddesses".
- **Demeter at Eleusis (mini wrath story / theoxeny):** disguised as an old woman, she is welcomed by king **Celeus**' daughters and queen **Metaneira**; **Iambe** (comic old servant) makes her laugh (→ ritual obscenities). She nurses baby **Demophoön**, putting him in the fire to make him immortal; Metaneira screams (offense) → Demeter enraged → no immortality, endless battle for Eleusis → appeasement: temple built, Mysteries founded.
- **Eleusinian Mysteries:** any Greek-speaking adult (men, women, slaves; truces in war); costs money; c. 1500 BCE – 392 CE, yearly near harvest. Days 1–2 Athens (sacred objects *hiera* brought to the Eleusinion); day 3 bathe in the sea (+ sacrifice a piglet); day 4 procession on the **Sacred Way** (branches, wreaths, torches, fasting, insults for Iambe); days 5–6 drink **kykeon** (barley drink), enter the **Telesterion** — things done, things shown (*hiera*), things said ("thrice blessed are those who have seen these rites"); hierophant; burst of light → **blessed afterlife**; days 7–8 sacrifice a bull, party for the gift of grain (**Triptolemus**); day 9 go home, **don't tell** (*mysterion* < *muo* "close the mouth"; Aeschylus allegedly tried for revealing them).
- **Demeter in literature vs. religion:** major in **religion** (initiation = essential part of being Greek), minor in literature (subordinated to Zeus).

## e2c2 — Mourning goddesses & "dying gods" (Powell 10 · Sept 29)
- **Syncretism** = combining religions, esp. equating foreign gods with Greek ones (*syn* "with") — e.g. **Isis = Demeter**. Not about demonizing foreign gods.
- **Isis & Osiris (Egypt):** Isis = daughter of Sky (Nut) + Earth (Geb), wife of **Osiris**, mother of **Horus**. Osiris killed and **dismembered by Seth** (≈ Typhoeus); Isis searches, collects the pieces. Allegory: Osiris = Nile / annual fertility; Pharaoh = Horus. Similarities to Demeter: grieving goddess searching, fertility, a mystery cult, return from death. *(Powell for the third point)*
- **Inanna (Ishtar) & Dumuzi (Tammuz):** Dumuzi = mortal lover of Inanna who dies, brought back one day/part of the year; sent to the underworld by angry Inanna.
- **Aphrodite & Adonis:** Adonis = Aphrodite's young lover (< Dumuzi); dies, mourned.
- **Cybele & Attis:** Cybele "the Great Mother", **Phrygian** fertility goddess; image: city-wall crown, cornucopia; controls wild animals; = Rhea/Demeter; born from Deucalion's rock. **Attis** = her lover/priest; marries king Midas' daughter; Cybele inflicts madness → **Attis castrates himself**; she collects/buries the pieces; his finger lives.
- **"Dying god" story** = Osiris, Dumuzi, Adonis, Attis, **Persephone** (unusual: female). Shared: fertility goddess, powerful, grief → search → resurrection, (dismemberment). Demeter & Persephone are atypical (dying *goddess*; Demeter maternal).
- Mourning images: Isis, Cybele, Demeter, Inanna, Aphrodite, Niobe, Mary (Pietà).

## e2c3 — Dionysus (Powell 11 · Oct 1 — no deck; review slides 23–31 + *Bacchae*)
- **Dionysus** = fertility god of wine; son of **Zeus and Semele** (daughter of Cadmus). Areas: wine, vegetation/life force, ecstasy/madness, theater, (afterlife in mysteries). Roman Bacchus. Signs in art: **ivy crown, wine cup (kantharos), thyrsus, leopard/panther skin, long robe**, beard (later youthful). Animals: **leopard/panther, bull, goat, snake, (lion)**; plants: **vine, ivy**. *(Powell)*
- Followers: female **maenads** ("mad women") = **Bacchae/bacchants** ("women of Bacchus") = **thyiads** ("raging ones") *(Powell — confirm 3 names)*; mortal women who follow Dionysus, carry the **thyrsus** (pine-cone-topped staff), perform **sparagmos** (ritual dismemberment) in myth. Male: **satyrs** (goat/horse-men: tails, ears, erect phalloi) and **Silenus**.
- **Family tree (CRQ):** Cadmus = Harmonia → **Semele** (+Zeus → Dionysus), **Autonoë** (→ Actaeon), **Agave** (+ Echion → **Pentheus**), **Ino** (+ Athamas). Dionysus later marries **Ariadne**. Semele destroyed by Zeus's brilliance (Hera's trick); Zeus sews the fetus into his thigh.
- **Divine visit (theoxeny):** the god who visits in person; test of virtue, usually in disguise; groups punished, individuals rewarded — **Dionysus & the pirates** (Homeric Hymn: vine, lion, sailors → dolphins, helmsman spared), Zeus & Lycaon, Aphrodite & Anchises, Demeter & Metaneira.
- **Resistance stories** (CRQ: give five) — **Pentheus** (Thebes), **Minyads** (princesses of Orchomenus who refuse, become **bats**), **Lycurgus** (Thrace), the **pirates**, **Proetids** (Argos), (the women of Argos/Athens). *(Powell — confirm list)* Wrath pattern again (Dionysus & Thebes).
- **King Midas:** Dionysus grants a wish → golden touch → washes it off in the Pactolus. *(Powell)*
- **Euripides, *Bacchae*** (Euripides c. 480–406 BCE; produced after his death, 405): set in **Thebes** before the palace; chorus = **bacchants** (Asian followers). Dionysus comes to Thebes first because his mother's sisters denied his divinity (Semele's story). **Pentheus** (young king, grandson of Cadmus) opposes him (thinks the rites are drunken sex), imprisons women and the god; **agon** (formal debate) with the disguised god — **dramatic irony** (audience knows he's the god). Palace **earthquake**, god released. On **Mt. Cithaeron** the women nurse animals, then tear apart cattle. Pentheus, dressed as a woman to spy, is **torn apart by his mother Agave** and aunts (sparagmos); she brings back his head thinking it's a lion's. Resolution: Cadmus & Harmonia exiled (to become snakes). Similar Theban fate: **Actaeon** (torn apart by his hounds).
- **Was Dionysus foreign? No** — in Greece since the 13th c. BCE (Linear B). "Foreign" = double identity (Thebes and abroad), masculine & feminine traits, **different from the Olympians**: he possesses his worshippers (enthusiasm = "filled with the god"), surrounded by humans. To early Christians: a rival — dying/reborn god, wine. *(Powell)*
- **Dionysus & Demeter:** male/female fertility counterparts (vine/grain), special link to humans (possession/initiation, mystery cults), joint festivals (**Haloa** at Athens), wrath stories; phalloi (life force).
- **Athens:** **City Dionysia** (tragedy, comedy, satyr plays) in the **Theater of Dionysus**; Lenaea, Anthesteria. *(Powell)*

## e2c4 — Death & the Underworld (Powell 12 · Oct 6)
- **Norms:** death rarely from old age; humans = soul + body; soul = breath (*psyche*, Lat. *anima*); souls live in Hades as grim, boring shades; can return briefly — friendly (Patroclus; race of gold/silver) or hostile (unburied dead, Erinyes). Festivals placate the angry dead (Anthesteria; Rome: Parentalia, Lemuria). Funerary rituals = **appease angry souls of the dead**.
- **Hades** names: Pluto (wealth), Dis, Aïdoneus, "the invisible one", Zeus of the underworld *(Powell — CRQ 2)*. **Charon** ferries souls across the **Styx**; **Cerberus**; **Hermes psychopompos** ("escorter of souls"). Judges: **Minos, Rhadamanthys, Aeacus**. River **Lethe** (forgetfulness).
- **Odyssey** (≈12,000 lines, by "Homer"): Odysseus sails Troy → Ithaca (10 yrs). **Book 11**, told to the Phaeacians (Arete & Alcinous). **Circe** sends him to consult the dead seer **Tiresias**; **necromancy** — trench, sacrifice, **blood** offering; souls drink, then speak.
  - **Elpenor** (crew member): got drunk on Circe's roof, fell, died — asks for burial ("do not leave me unwept and unburied").
  - **Tiresias:** how to get home, dangers (Scylla, Charybdis, the suitors).
  - **Anticlea** (his mother, daughter of Autolycus): he tries 3× to hug her — only a soul; advice: Penelope is loyal, **GET HOME**.
  - **Agamemnon:** murdered by his wife **Clytemnestra** (+ Cassandra) → wives can be evil.
  - **Ajax:** still angry about Achilles' armor, won't speak.
  - **Achilles:** "better to serve as a hired man to a landless man than rule over all the dead" → afterlife is bad, enjoy life.
  - Purpose: **motivate him to get home**.
- **Sinners:** **Tantalus** (fed son Pelops to the gods → hunger/thirst, food & water recede), **Sisyphus** (cheated death → boulder uphill forever), **Ixion** (assaulted Hera → fiery wheel), **Danaids** (killed husbands → carry water in a sieve/leaky jars). Unusual crimes against gods — no reward/punishment for most people.
- **Elysium / Isles of the Blessed:** like Hesiod's golden age, ruled by Cronus; heroes (Menelaus in the *Odyssey*; later Achilles, Diomedes, Cadmus); later an "Elysium within Hades" for initiates of Demeter, Dionysus, Orpheus.
- **Orpheus:** son of a Muse (Calliope) + Oeagrus; famous singer; wife **Eurydice** dies of a snakebite on the wedding day; **katabasis**; sings for Hades & Persephone; prohibition: don't look back → loses her; killed by **maenads** in **Thrace** (Dionysus hostile / or rival cult; associated with Apollo). Told by **Virgil, *Georgics* 4** (farming poems) and Ovid *Met.* 10–11.
- **Orphism:** religious/philosophical movement. **Cosmogony:** Time → **cosmic egg** → **Phanes** ("appearer") → then Uranus, Gaea… (follows Hesiod after that); alternate Dionysus myth (Dionysus dismembered by Titans, cf. Osiris). Afterlife: **reincarnation** (drink from Lethe, new bodies); strict living purifies → reward = escape the cycle / **blessed afterlife**. **Plato's Myth of Er** (*Republic*): souls choose their next lives. *(Powell for Er details)*
- **Aeneid** (Virgil, 70–19 BCE; ~10,000-line Latin epic): **Aeneas**, Trojan hero (son of Aphrodite & Anchises) → Italy → founds Rome. **Book 6:** helped by the **Sibyl of Cumae** (guide) and the **golden bough** (passport); meets **Palinurus** (dead helmsman ≈ Elpenor), **Dido** (hates him, silent ≈ Ajax), **Deiphobus** (killed by his own wife's betrayal ≈ Agamemnon), father **Anchises** (≈ Anticlea) who shows him future Romans: "Roman, remember by your strength to rule… to spare the conquered, battle down the proud." Message: let go of the past, Roman values, Elysium for all the virtuous.
- **Katabasis** ("descent to the underworld") — Dumuzi, Persephone, Odysseus, Orpheus, Heracles, Theseus, Aeneas (+ joke version: Dionysus in Aristophanes' *Frogs*). Common theme: **they come back alive**; later heroes *need* one (legitimacy). Why each went: see `03-lecture-questions-answer-key.md` B14.

## e2c5 — Gilgamesh & the idea of the hero (Powell 13 · Oct 8 — no deck; review slides 44–55)
- **Hero (*hērōs*)** to classical Greeks: a dead man of the past with special status, worshipped at his tomb — mixed parentage, raised in the wild, divine protectors/opponents. Examples: Achilles, Heracles, Theseus, Oedipus, Perseus.
- **Hero cult** (from c. 800 BCE): offerings, prayers, requests at the tomb (tumulus → later **heroon**); physical connection (bones — Athenians fetched Theseus' bones from Scyros; Spartans stole Orestes' bones). Heroes were **worshipped** (not deified) to **seek their help**.
- **Gilgamesh** = king of **Uruk**, legendary hero of Mesopotamia (⅔ god); *Epic of Gilgamesh* = oldest heroic epic (cuneiform; Standard Babylonian version). Opening like the *Odyssey*: a poem about "a man" who traveled and gained knowledge.
- **Enkidu:** created by **Aruru** (mother goddess) from clay as a companion/rival because Gilgamesh abuses his power; wild man running with animals. A trapper (on Gilgamesh's advice) brings the **prostitute/priestess Shamhat** → after sex he eats bread, drinks beer/wine, wears clothes; animals flee → civilized. Comes to Uruk, they **wrestle, then become friends**; Enkidu = subordinate partner/helper. **Hero's companion** (cf. Achilles & Patroclus).
- **Humbaba:** monster guarding the **cedar forest**; long journey; they kill him (offer head to Enlil, who is angry). Theme: nature vs. culture.
- **Ishtar** proposes; Gilgamesh refuses, listing her ruined lovers (Dumuzi…) → she has Anu send the **Bull of Heaven** → they kill it (Enkidu throws its thigh at her) — a wrath story. Humbaba & Bull: both monsters killed by the pair, both anger gods; difference: Humbaba is a quest they choose / the Bull is sent against them.
- **Enkidu dies** (gods' punishment) → Gilgamesh's grief; fear of death; dark Mesopotamian underworld (dust, clay for food) ≈ *Odyssey* 11 gloom.
- **Siduri** (ale-wife): enjoy life — eat, drink, love your wife and child (cf. Achilles' message). Crosses the waters of death to **Utnapishtim** (flood survivor, given immortality). Tests fail: can't stay awake (**loaves of bread** mark the days) and the **prickly herb** of youth is stolen by a **snake** → common theme: **humans cannot escape death**. Returns to Uruk, admires the walls.
- Examples in Gilgamesh (CRQ): wrath (Ishtar/Bull), etiology (snake sheds skin), dying god (Dumuzi mentioned), katabasis (Enkidu's dream/visit; crossing waters of death).
- **Gilgamesh & Odysseus:** travel, cross waters of death, strange women, offer of immortality from a goddess, gain knowledge, message = mortality, return home and rule.
- **Hero-myth pattern** (Powell): divine parent; unusual birth; great strength, menace to his people; male companion; impossible labors; breaks a taboo; resists an enticing woman; responsible for a companion's death; journey to the otherworld; (cult after death). Heroes relate to gods differently: divine parent/protector, divine opponent.
- Parallel pairs + theory (CRQ): Greek ↔ Mesopotamian myths (succession, flood, Achilles/Patroclus) — borrowing via trade/contact in the Near East (orientalizing period) or common inheritance.

## e2c6 — Perseus & the Argive plain (Powell 14 · Oct 13)
- **Argive plain (Argolid):** Mycenae (Agamemnon; Lion Gate c. 1250 BCE; grave circle; "mask of Agamemnon"), Argos, Heraeum (Io), Lerna (Hydra), river Inachus. Genealogies systematize myths with **eponymous ancestors** (Nilus, Memphis, Libya, Aegyptus, Europa).
- **Io:** daughter of river **Inachus**, priestess of **Hera**; Zeus sleeps with her (unwelcome); turned into a **cow**, given to Hera; guarded by **Argus** (100 eyes); **Hermes kills Argus** ("Argeiphontes"); Hera's **gadfly** drives her across the world; visits **Prometheus** in the Caucasus (principal account: **Aeschylus, *Prometheus Bound***); ends in **Egypt**, restored, bears Epaphus.
- **Danaids:** 50 daughters of Danaus forced to marry 50 sons of Aegyptus; **49 kill their husbands, 1 (Hypermnestra) spares hers** ("all but one" folktale motif); punished in the underworld carrying water in leaky jars; etiology of Lerna's springs (Amymone & Poseidon).
- **Girl's tragedy** pattern: high-status girl → prohibition (father) → seclusion → violation (often forcible, by a god) → male child → discovered (accused of lying) → punished → liberated/compensated → child recognized. Examples: Io, **Danaë**, Medusa (late), **Antiope**, Tyro, Callisto, Rhea Silvia, Auge, Amymone, Kreousa (Daphne = failed).
- **Danaë:** daughter of **Acrisius**, king of Argos (prophecy: grandson will kill him) → locked up (bronze chamber) → **Zeus as golden rain** → **Perseus** → mother & baby in a **chest at sea** → washes up on **Seriphos**, **Dictys** (kind fisherman) raises them; his brother **Polydectes** (bad king) wants Danaë, demands a gift (horse) → Perseus promises Medusa's head. Simonides' lyric poem: Danaë singing to baby Perseus — very human hero.
- **Gorgons:** three sisters (Medusa mortal); big eyes, snake hair, tusks; **apotropaic** (ward off evil) faces on shields (*gorgoneion*) **before** Perseus' myth. **Medusa** (< Eurymedousa "wide-ruling"), in a late version a priestess of Athena violated by Poseidon and turned into a monster; killed while pregnant → **Pegasus** (and Chrysaor) born from her neck.
- **Helpers/gear:** Athena (guidance, mirror-shield), Hermes (sickle/sword), the **Graeae** ("grey ones", three sisters sharing one eye & tooth → give directions), nymphs give **cap of Hades** (invisibility), **winged sandals**, **magic pouch (kibisis)**. Uses the shield as a mirror, cuts off the head, flies away.
- **Andromeda** (principal account: **Ovid, *Metamorphoses* 4**): daughter of **Cepheus & Cassiopea** (Ethiopia / Joppa); Cassiopea boasts → **Nereids** offended → Poseidon sends **Ceto** (sea monster) → Andromeda chained to a rock; Perseus kills it (sword); Medusa's head on seaweed creates **coral**; rival suitor Phineus (Cepheus cheats) → turned to stone.
- **Return:** Polydectes turned to stone; **Dictys becomes king**. Perseus kills **Acrisius accidentally with a discus** (prophecy fulfilled), takes Tiryns, **founds Mycenae**. Athena gets the head (aegis). After death: Perseus, Andromeda, Cepheus, Cassiopea, Ceto → **constellations**. Cult at Argos, Mycenae, Seriphos.
- **Folktale motifs:** outcast, magic items, witches (Graeae), wicked king, three sisters, kills monster, wins princess, wins kingdom, happy ending (cf. Cinderella, Jack and the Beanstalk). Unlike Achilles, Perseus **dies happily in old age**.

## e2c7 — Thebes: Cadmus, Oedipus & sons (Powell 18 · Oct 15)
- Thebes is unique: **founded twice**; our main source is **Greek tragedy**; city of incest & fratricide.
- **Europa:** Phoenician princess (daughter of **Agenor**); **Zeus as a bull** carries her to **Crete** → Minos, Sarpedon, Rhadamanthys; **eponymous ancestor of Europe**; girl's tragedy.
- **Cadmus** (Phoenician, "man from the east") sent to find Europa → **Delphi**: stop searching, **follow a cow**, found a city where it lies down → needs water for sacrifice → **dragon/serpent of Ares** guards the spring, kills his men (bites, crushes, poison breath) → Cadmus kills it (vase: rock; **Ovid**: boulder, then javelin, **pins it to an oak**) = Indo-European **dragon slaying** (overcoming death/chaos; cf. Marduk, Zeus–Typhoeus, Apollo–Python, Perseus, Heracles). **Athena**: sow the teeth → **Spartoi** ("sown men") fight → **five survive** → five noble families. Punished: serves **Ares 8 years**, then marries his daughter **Harmonia**.
- **Doomed wedding** (gods attend; resentful god; cursed gift): Cadmus & Harmonia — **necklace (by Hephaestus) and robe**; cf. Peleus & Thetis (Eris, **apple of discord**), Pirithous & Hippodamia (centauromachy). Cadmus & Harmonia end as **snakes → Elysium**. Cadmus linked to the alphabet (*kadmeia*); Mesopotamian cylinder seals found at Thebes.
- **Daughters:** **Ino** (nurses Dionysus; Hera maddens Athamas; leaps into the sea → Leucothea), **Semele** (Dionysus; burned by Zeus), **Agave** (kills Pentheus), **Autonoë** (mother of **Actaeon**, who sees Artemis bathing → deer → torn apart by his hounds).
- **Second founding — Amphion & Zethus:** sons of **Antiope** by Zeus (girl's tragedy ×2: kicked out by father → marries king of Sicyon → uncle Lycus kills him, reclaims her → tortured by aunt **Dirce** → finds her twins (raised by a shepherd) → they kill Dirce (tied to a bull)). **Amphion** (marries **Niobe**) **sings** stones into walls; **Zethus** (marries Thebe — eponym) **works**. Reconciled: Cadmus built the citadel (Cadmeia, people), the twins built the walls / lower city. Double foundings: Rome (Aeneas; Romulus & Remus), Troy (Ilus; Poseidon & Apollo's walls).
- **Oedipus** ("swollen foot"): **Laius** (king of Thebes) = **Jocasta**; oracle: don't have a son / son will kill you → Laius disobeys, exposes the baby on **Mt. Cithaeron** (ankles pinned) → shepherd gives him to **Polybus & Merope** of **Corinth** (raised in a city = unheroic). Learns he might be adopted → **Delphi** → same prophecy → leaves Corinth → kills a stranger (Laius) at a crossroads → **Sphinx** (woman/lion, kills travelers to Thebes) → solves the **riddle** → reward = queen Jocasta → king. Children: **Eteocles, Polynices, Antigone, Ismene**. **Creon** = Jocasta's brother.
- **Sophocles, *Oedipus the King*** (Sophocles 496–406 BCE; 7 tragedies survive incl. *OT*, *Oedipus at Colonus*, *Antigone*): plague (**miasma**) → asks Delphi via Creon → proclamation to find Laius' killer → questions **Tiresias**, Creon, Jocasta, the messenger from Corinth, the herdsman → truth → **Jocasta hangs herself**, Oedipus **blinds himself** (with her brooches), exile. Themes: being vs. appearance (sighted but blind / blind Tiresias sees; king but polluted; savior is the plague's cause), ignorance = mortal condition, no escaping fate (*moira*). Oedipus polluted but not corrupted → cult honors.
- **Oedipus at Colonus** (406 BCE): blind Oedipus guided by his **daughters (Antigone, Ismene)** to Colonus near Athens; **Theseus protects him**; he **blesses his daughters, curses his sons**; dies, receives **hero cult** (heroon).
- **Sons:** agree to alternate rule; **Eteocles won't share** → **Polynices** to **Argos**, marries a daughter of king **Adrastus** (Tydeus marries the other); bribes **Eriphyle** with **Harmonia's necklace** to make her husband **Amphiaraus** (a seer who foresaw death) join → Eriphyle proverbial for treachery. **Seven Against Thebes** (Aeschylus, 467 BCE): 7 attackers vs. 7 defenders at 7 gates; brothers **kill each other** (civil war); only **Adrastus** survives (horse Arion). Tydeus dies (eats Melanippus' brain → Athena withholds immortality).
- **Antigone** (Sophocles, 441 BCE): new king **Creon** forbids burying Polynices → Antigone buries him (family/divine law vs. state) → buried alive → hangs herself; Haemon and Eurydice also die.

## e2c8 — Athens & Theseus (Powell 16 · Oct 20)
- Athens had only minor heroes until the mid-6th c. → **Pisistratus** (and later Miltiades, Cimon) promoted Theseus as a "new Heracles" — **myth as propaganda** (epic *Theseïs*; shrine for his bones from Scyros). But also really believed: had a shrine.
- **Athenian origin claims:** (1) **autochthony** ("born from the soil" = being indigenous), (2) descent from **Athena**, (3) descent from **Cecrops** (half-snake, earthborn king).
- **Erichthonius** ("wool" + "earth"): Hephaestus pursues Athena; his semen falls on her thigh → she wipes it with wool → Earth (Gaea) bears the child → Athena puts him in a **covered basket** given to Cecrops' daughters **Aglauros, Herse, Pandrosos** with a prohibition → two look (see a snake/snake-child), go mad, **leap from the Acropolis**; Pandrosos obeys. = wrath story & **etiology** of the **Arrhephoria** (two girls, the *arrhephoroi*, live a year on the Acropolis, weave Athena's robe, carry secret baskets). Erichthonius becomes king (wooden statue of Athena). Same story type: Pandora's jar / forbidden look.
- **Royal house:** Erichthonius → Pandion → **Erechtheus**, **Procne & Philomela**; Erechtheus → **Procris** (m. Cephalus), Creusa, Orithyia; **Herse + Hermes → Cephalus**; Eos (Dawn) loves Cephalus; Phaethon. Two Pandions, two Praxitheas — hard to reconcile.
- **Procris & Cephalus** (told by Ovid; moral: jealousy/lack of trust; Ovid not serious): mutual infidelity tests; Procris flees to Crete, cures **Minos** (Pasiphaë's curse), gets the hound **Laelaps** and a never-missing **javelin** → reconciled; she spies on Cephalus while he hunts ("Aura" breeze), he kills her by accident with the javelin.
- **Tereus, Procne, Philomela** (Ovid *Met.* 6): Thracian king **Tereus** (son of Ares) marries Athenian **Procne** (daughter of **Pandion**); fetches her sister **Philomela**, **rapes her**, cuts out her tongue; she **weaves** her story; Procne kills their son **Itys** and feeds him to Tereus (hesitates—Itys resembles his father; cf. Medea, Agave, Tantalus); all become birds (nightingale, swallow, **hoopoe** = Tereus). Main vice: **lust**. Thracian stereotype; bigamy ends badly.
- **Theseus' birth:** **Aegeus** (king of Athens, childless, married to **Medea**) asks **Delphi** → riddle ("do not loosen the wineskin's foot until you reach Athens") → **Pittheus** of **Troezen** understands it, gets him drunk with his daughter **Aethra**; **Poseidon** also sleeps with her → Theseus has **three parents**. Aegeus leaves **sword & sandals under a rock**; when Theseus can lift it he goes to Athens.
- **Labors on the road** (≈ Heracles, but volunteered): **Periphetes** (club-man; takes his club), **Sinis** (pine-bender → torn by pines), **Crommyonian sow**, **Sciron** (makes travelers wash his feet, kicks them off a cliff to a turtle → same fate), **Cercyon** (wrestler → wrestled to death), **Procrustes** (stretches/cuts guests to fit his bed → same), (later the **Bull of Marathon**).
- **In Athens:** **Medea** (witch, Aegeus' wife) tries to kill him — the Bull of Marathon, then **poison**; Aegeus **recognizes the sword**; Pallas' 50 sons (nephews) attack, killed. → Minotaur (Crete, e2c9). **Black sails**: forgets to change to white → **Aegeus leaps** (Aegean Sea). King of Athens; unifier (synoikism), coins.
- **Amazons** (daughters of Ares; "a-mazos" "without a breast" → said to cut off the right breast; invert gender norms): Theseus abducts **Antiope/Hippolyta** → Amazons invade Athens → **Amazonomachy** (on the Parthenon) = symbol of Greek victory over the **Persians** (after 490 BCE).
- **Hippolytus** (son of Theseus & the Amazon) — **Euripides, *Hippolytus***: devoted to **Artemis**, scorns **Aphrodite** → she makes stepmother **Phaedra** (daughter of Minos) love him; Phaedra ashamed; **Nurse** tells him; he's disgusted/overreacts; Phaedra hangs herself leaving a letter accusing him; Theseus curses him via **Poseidon** → bull from the sea → killed by his horses ("hippo-lytus" = undone by horses); Artemis reveals the truth. Euripides' change: Phaedra doesn't proposition him directly (sanitized). **Potiphar's wife** tale type (false rape accusation): Joseph (Genesis 39), **Bellerophon** (Stheneboea, Proetus → Chimera, Pegasus), **Peleus** (wife of Acastus), Hippolytus.
- **Pirithous** (king of the **Lapiths**, son of Ixion): fight → friends (cf. Gilgamesh & Enkidu); his wedding → **centauromachy**; pact to marry daughters of Zeus: Theseus abducts child **Helen** (left with Aethra; her brothers the Dioscuri rescue her and **enslave Aethra**); Pirithous wants **Persephone** → katabasis → stuck on chairs of forgetfulness; **Heracles rescues Theseus**, Pirithous stays forever.
- **End:** Menestheus takes Athens; Theseus flees to **Scyros**, king **Lycomedes pushes him off a cliff** (like his father's fall). Cimon brings the bones back (5th c.).

## e2c9 — Crete: Minos, the Minotaur, Daedalus (Powell 17 · Oct 22)
- Myths of Athens told by Athenians; myths of Crete also told by **Athenians** (6th–5th c., 600+ years after the palaces fell) — surviving versions mostly later (Ovid, Apollodorus). Athens looks good; Thebes, Thrace, Corinth, **Crete** look bad.
- **Minoans** (from Minos; 7000–1450 BCE): unfortified palaces (**Cnossus** — excavated by **Arthur Evans** from 1899/1900; Phaistos, Malia, Zakros); wealth & trade (pithoi); naturalistic frescoes; **bull-leaping** (fresco, Powell 17.8) → link to Theseus & the Minotaur; goddess worship (**snake goddess**, faience) → Ariadne a demoted goddess? ("very holy"), Pasiphaë ("bright") a moon goddess? Writing: **Linear A** (undeciphered), **Linear B** (Greek, administrative only). End: Thera eruption, **Mycenaean Greeks take over c. 1450 BCE**, final destruction c. 1200.
- **Agenor** rules Phoenicia (Tyre/Sidon); son **Cadmus** founds Thebes; daughter **Europa** stolen by Zeus (bull) — Ovid: "grandeur and love do not go well together" (Zeus lowers himself). In **Crete** she bears **Minos, Sarpedon, Rhadamanthys**; gifts: **Laelaps** (dog), **Talos** (bronze giant), a javelin; marries Asterion.
- **Minos' family:** wife **Pasiphaë** (daughter of **Helios** & Perseis; sister of **Circe** and **Aeëtes** (father of **Medea**)); children **Ariadne, Phaedra, Androgeus**, (Glaucus, Deucalion).
- **Cretan bull wrath story:** Minos claims the throne, prays to **Poseidon** for a bull to sacrifice → keeps it → Poseidon's wrath → **Pasiphaë** lusts for the bull → **Daedalus** builds a **hollow wooden cow** → **Minotaur** ("bull of Minos", Asterius) → Daedalus builds the **labyrinth** at **Cnossus**. Ovid, ***Ars Amatoria* 1**: Pasiphaë's color-coordinated outfits, mirror ("you're not a Holstein"), has her rival cows sacrificed; longs to be Europa or Io. Shared trait of Minos' wife & daughters: **disastrous passion**. Heracles takes the bull to Greece → **Bull of Marathon**.
- **Minos vs. Athens:** son **Androgeus** wins the Panathenaic games, killed by the **Marathonian bull** (Aegeus sent him) → Minos takes **Megara** (**Scylla** cuts her father **Nisus**' purple hair — folktale, like Samson; Minos rejects her) → can't take Athens → **Zeus sends plague** → tribute of **7 youths + 7 maidens** every 9 years for the Minotaur.
- **Theseus proves divine birth:** on the ship Minos throws a **ring** into the sea; Theseus dives, **Amphitrite** (Poseidon's wife) gives him the ring and a wreath/crown.
- **Labyrinth:** **Ariadne** (in love) gives the **thread/clew** (Daedalus' idea) → kills the Minotaur with a sword (vases never show her handing over the thread). Abandons Ariadne on **Naxos** (sleeping) → **Dionysus rescues/marries her** (crown → constellation). Later marries **Phaedra** → Hippolytus disaster.
- **Minotaur story = folktale type:** travel, wicked king, human sacrifice, princess helps, defeats monster, wins princess (Jason & Medea, Perseus & Andromeda, Iphigenia among the Taurians, Euripides' *Helen*). Folklore elements vs. heroic legend: needs **connection with a named place** (cult) to become legend.
- **Daedalus:** Athenian craftsman; killed his nephew/apprentice **Perdix** (inventor of the saw from a fish spine / compass) → turned into a **partridge** → Daedalus banished → Crete. Minos exploits Athenian talent (≠ Theseus, good king who protects suppliants: Oedipus, Antigone, Orestes). Imprisoned after helping Ariadne → **wings** of feathers & wax ("adversity sharpens the mind", Ovid *Met.* 8); **Icarus** "overly rash in his adolescent bravado" flies too high → wax melts → falls (Icarian Sea) — like Phaethon; moral: moderation / obey your father (Ovid not always serious; persecuted artist like Ovid). Last accomplishment: threads a **spiral conch shell** (thread tied to an ant) at the court of **Cocalus** in **Sicily** → betrays himself → Cocalus' daughters **boil/scald Minos in his bath**. Athens wins.
- **Pre-Athenian Minos:** wise king & **lawgiver** (Homer, Hesiod), Zeus's friend, **judge in the underworld** with Rhadamanthys & Aeacus.

|---|---|---|
| **Wrath story** (offense → anger → destruction → appeasement → new ritual) | menis of a god | Demeter & Persephone; Demeter at Eleusis; Dionysus & Thebes (Pentheus); Apollo & Agamemnon (*Iliad* 1); Ishtar & the Bull of Heaven; Poseidon & Minos (Cretan bull); Athena & Cecrops' daughters (Erichthonius); Poseidon & Cassiopea |
