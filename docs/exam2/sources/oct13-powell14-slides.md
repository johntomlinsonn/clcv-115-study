--- Slide 13 ---
Simonides’ Poem about Perseus 
6th-5th c. lyric poet
poem for dancers to sing
= Danaë singing to baby Perseus
The crash of the breakers over your head,
the driving waves and the hurrying wind,
mean nothing to you. So snuggle down 
in your warm dark blanket, upon my breast.
My heart would break if you knew that this storm
was a threat to you, if your baby ears
   might understand my terrified words. 
			Simonides, fr. 38, Powell, Classical Myth, p. 345)
