NOTES: What happens after you die? We all want to know. It’s almost universal to fear death – for Greeks especially. They thought the afterlife was a grim, boring place. Exceptions for some people (Elysian fields, initiates of Demeter and Persephone). Maybe that’s why they told so many stories about people who beat the odds. People who went head to head with Hades, and won. 
 
It’s like disaster stories. We want to hear about the survivors.

this is NOT the Spurlock krater (similar, has the Danaids, Persephone is pulling aside her veil) Tarantine Greek red figure vase showing the underworld. Hades and Persephone hold court in the centre with Aeacus, a judge of the virtues of the deceased, sits to their right. In the foreground Cerberus can be seen in the centre with Sisyphus to his left and Tantalus to his right. Orpheus is playing his lyre next to Persephone. The person holding Cerberus is probably Heracles. (Photo by Culture Club/Getty Images)

One way we know it’s Persephone and Hades is from the surrounding figures. Furies are dressed as huntresses (< theoi.com): conceived as hunters. Heracles has his lion skin and club. Hecate has a torch and panther skin, dressed as a huntress. Cerberus has a snake tail.

 “Numb dead people

BUT seems to contradict the rationale for heroic achievement in the Iliad (eternal kleos) by suggesting that the individual in no way benefits, a question the Iliad itself raised, although not so forcefully. Here nostos is prized over kleos. Achilles had a choice between the two. But Odysseus is winning both.
 
This may show signs of borrowing:
Cf. Gilgamesh: Standard Babylonian Version also a crossing of the waters of death, search for knowledge, discovery that there is no immortality; forced to take Siduri’s advice and live for the here and now. 
Alt. version, Gilgamesh communicates with the dead Enkidu.
Alt. version, Enkidu makes a trip to the underworld for Gilgamesh. In both Gilgamesh learns the same thing: everything is dust (cf. ‘you are lost in the dust and cannot hear me’).
 
Why borrowing?
Achilles’ description doesn’t fit Greek conception of the underworld.
 
hero cult presumes a kind of dwelling in/near the tomb which becomes a shrine where one leaves offerings (us. a monument, better to have hero’s bones). Hero cult means special status after death, Menelaos an example. Achilles is too (stolen by Thetis on his death, taken to the White Island, id’d on Black Sea, cult attested).
Hades also generally includes an area of torment (as here, for Sisyphus, Tantalus) and the Elysian fields (blessed existence). No sign of the latter.
 
Extreme pessimism interpreted as characteristic of a different religious world view. Possibly an inheritance.
