Cybele (Phrygian fertility goddess, her priest is Attis)
Demeter (fertility goddess of crops, mother of Persephone)

Eros (Love, primordial force/son of Aphrodite)

Isis (Egyptian goddess, wife of Osiris, mother of Horus)
Medusa (woman turned into a snake-haired monster, her stare turns to stone)
Minotaur (half man/half bull child of Minos and Pasiphae)
Nymphs (minor goddesses of nature)

Concepts/Items
Apple of Discord (at wedding of Peleus and Thetis)
Autochthony (means being indigenous) (Athenians’ belief that they were born from the soil of Athens)
“Escorter of souls” (psychopompos, epithet of Hermes)
Anthropomorphism (giving human traits to gods/other nonhumans)
Apotropaic (‘warding off evil,’ the function e.g., of a herm) 
Centauromachy (fight at the wedding of Pirithous, against the Lapiths, a group of mortals from Thessaly)
“Descent to the underworld” story (katabasis, performed by Odysseus, Heracles, Theseus, Aeneas)
Divine visit (theoxeny) story (Dionysus & pirates, Demeter and royal family at Eleusis)
“Doomed wedding” (e.g., Peleus and Thetis, Cadmus and Harmonia)
“Dying god” story (Osiris, Dumuzi, Adonis, Attis, Persephone)
Eleusinian Mysteries (special ritual of Demeter and Persephone that brought benefits in the afterlife)
Elysium (good part of the Underworld, to reward the virtuous)
Eponymous ancestor (the origin of a name, e.g., Europa and Europe)
Etiological story (explains how something came to be, e.g., why Apollo has a shrine on Delos)
Folk tale (stories about ordinary people, talking animals)
Folk tale motifs in Perseus story (magic items, three sisters, etc.)
Food of the Dead (e.g., pomegranate, Persephone story)
Girl’s Tragedy (story type of rape by god, illegitimate child, punishment, etc.)
Greek beliefs about death and the underworld
Harmonia’s necklace (gift of Hephaestus, cursed)
Hero (= heros, Greek concept) (mixed parentage, brought up in wild, divine protectors/opponents)
Hero cult (worship at shrines, after death)
Hero’s companion (Enkidu, Patroclus)
Hospitality (xenia) (obligations of courteous treatment between guests and hosts)
Judgement of Paris (shepherd decides the most beautiful goddess, starts the Trojan war)
Justice (dikê, proper treatment of others, enforced by Zeus)
Labyrinth (maze built by Daedalus to house the Minotaur)
Legends (about human heroes, set in the past, e.g., Perseus)
Myth (a traditional story with collective importance, especially about gods)
Orphism (a cosmogony = theory of creation of the universe, born from an egg + reincarnation)
Potiphar’s wife folk tale type (a false rape accusation, e.g., Hippolytus, Bellerophon)
Ritual dismemberment (sparagmos) (performed by maenads in myth)
Succession myth (a tale type exemplified in Hesiod’s Theogony, with Uranus, Cronus, and Zeus)
Syncretism (“blending,” Greek practice of identifying foreign gods with their own, e.g., that Isis was a version of Demeter) 
Thyrsus (pine-cone topped staff, held by maenads)
Underworld (realm of Hades, where souls go after death, has famous sinners)
Wrath Story (story type, offense against a god, anger, destruction, appeasement, new ritual)
Places (be able to identify them on a map)
Argive plain (location of Mycenae, Argos, river Inachus)
Athens (Athena’s city)
Corinth (home of Oedipus’ foster parents, Polybus and Merope)
Crete (home of Minos and the Minotaur)
Delphi (shrine and oracle of Apollo)
Eleusis (township in Athens, site of sanctuary of Demeter)
Greece
Mesopotamia
Mt. Olympus (home of the 12 Olympian gods)
Thebes (home of the House of Cadmus)
